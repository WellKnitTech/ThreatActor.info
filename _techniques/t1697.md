## Description

Adversaries may steal Application Specific Passwords (ASPs) to gain access to Valid Accounts, bypassing multifactor authentication (MFA) methods. ASPs are passwords typically used to give third-party applications or legacy devices (which do not support MFA methods) account access. [Application Specific Passwords](https://astrix.security/learn/blog/app-specific-passwords-origins-functionality-security-risks/) [Application Specific Passwords Push Sec](https://pushsecurity.com/blog/app-specific-password-phishing) 
 
An adversary may socially engineer a user into providing an ASP [UNC6293 ASP Creative Phishing Attack](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/creative-phishing-academics-critics-of-russia) [Application Specific Passwords Phishing](https://citizenlab.ca/research/russian-government-linked-social-engineering-targets-app-specific-passwords/) (using methods like Phishing for Information), generate an ASP for an already compromised account, [Mandiant APT42-untangling](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/untangling-iran-apt42-operations) [TAG APT42](https://blog.google/threat-analysis-group/iranian-backed-group-steps-up-phishing-campaigns-against-israel-us/) or obtain an ASP stored on a system. Afterward, adversaries may use the ASP to access Valid Accounts and, in some cases, maintain Persistence, bypassing any form of MFA set on the account.
